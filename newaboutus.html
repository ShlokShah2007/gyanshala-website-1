Gyan Shala was founded by Prof. Pankaj Jain, alongside colleagues from IIMA and IRMA, to develop and implement a highly effective, high-quality education model for India's underserved children at scale.

The initiative was sparked by Prof. Jain's experience on the terminal review mission for the Andhra Pradesh Primary Education Project (APPEP) in the early 90s. Despite being well-funded and professionally executed, the British-backed program failed to produce significant improvements in student learning outcomes.

"The traditional model of quality schooling, which is heavily dependent on exceptional teachers led by exceptional leaders, could realistically serve only the top 5% of children."

During his other work, Prof. Jain observed that Bangladeshi girls from poor families were outperforming their Indian counterparts in early schooling, despite Bangladesh being economically poorer and socially more conservative at the time. These observations challenged his fundamental understanding of pedagogy.

Throughout the mid-to-late 90s, he studied various government and non-government school programs in India and many other countries. His research led to a critical insight — for the remaining 95%, a different approach was needed: one that ensures every child meets clearly defined learning outcomes while operating with readily available and sustainable human and financial resources at scale.

Drawing inspiration from excellent schooling systems and large-scale development programs such as AMUL and BRAC, Prof. Jain and his colleagues founded Gyan Shala under the Education Support Organization.

How We Work

Our Approach

The system of education that Prof. Jain developed has four distinct features, each designed to systematically overcome the barriers that prevent quality schooling from reaching every child.

Child-Centric Focus

More focus on children's capability to learn rather than on enhancing the teacher's capability to teach.

Team-Based Teaching

Reengineering the teacher's role into a four-tiered team effort, bringing curriculum design close to the classroom.

System Organization

Taking an overall system perspective to design a school program, rather than focusing only on teaching and learning.

Independent Assessment

Periodic assessments by reputed external agencies (like EI using ASSET tests) to gauge effectiveness and drive continuous improvement.

Ensuring Regularity

Students stay in school by locating classes near homes, maintaining a positive environment, and providing all materials free of charge.

Teacher Support

Teachers receive extensive program-specific training and supervisory support visits twice a week, ensuring consistent quality.

The 5A Implementation

Overcoming socio-economic barriers to reach every child

Awareness

Behavioral change towards education through continuous community engagement and motivation.

Availability

Ensuring quality schooling is present in every slum cluster, making education available to all marginalized children.

Accessibility

Equitable residential education held within the community (walking distance) with suitable timings for students.

Affordability

No indirect costs — books, transport, or stationeries — removing barriers for poor families.

Accountability

Daily evaluations and external assessments guided by dedicated MEL staff for course correction.

Our 1,200-person team — teachers, field supervisors, and curriculum designer-cum-managers — ensures implementation. Classrooms follow best practices including whole class teaching, small group activities for peer learning, and self-learning through daily workbooks.

Who We Are

Our Team Structure

Gyan Shala operates through a specialized six-tier structure. This model deconstructs the traditional role of a single teacher into a team-based effort, ensuring curriculum design, field support, and classroom delivery work in perfect harmony.

1
CEO

Coordinate with all Team Leads to manage cross-city programs, handle donor relations, fundraise, and ensure the long-term sustainability of the organization.

2
Team Leaders

Instruct and coordinate the curriculum design teams, evaluate study materials, and serve as the vital link between academic operations and executive leadership.

3
Curriculum Designers

Architect the student workbooks and teacher guides. They transform high-level goals into daily lessons and iterate materials based on field feedback.

4
Senior Supervisors

Lead monthly supervisor meetings, prepare staff for teacher training, and bridge the gap between the Curriculum team and field implementation. They oversee supervisors and conduct regular field visits.

5
Supervisors

Provide on-ground support to teachers in conducting classes, assist with community fieldwork, and attend monthly alignment meetings to maintain instructional quality.

6
Class Teachers

Gyan Shala replaces the traditional single-teacher model with a robust "Chain of Support," ensuring our 1,200+ frontline educators are backed by layers of academic, supervisory, and strategic expertise.
